On October 3–4, 2026, [Stéphane Chaillou](/team/stephane-chaillou/) and
[Alice Lima](/team/alice-lima/) took part in the **Village des sciences at
ENS Paris-Saclay** for the Fête de la science, France's annual science festival.
This year's theme, **“Saveurs savantes”**, invited visitors to explore the
science behind food and flavour.

The [Paris-Saclay science village](https://ens-paris-saclay.fr/agenda/fete-de-la-science-paris-saclay-2026)
was held in the school's Germaine Tillion atrium in Gif-sur-Yvette. Led by
La Diagonale at Université Paris-Saclay, with the participation of Plateforme 37,
it brought together local research laboratories for interactive activities
and short talks across biology, chemistry, physics and other disciplines.
Following a day for school groups on October 2, the weekend welcomed the
general public free of charge.

Together with **Éric Dugat-Bony and Gwendoline Marquet from SAYFOOD**, partners
in the [MetaSimFood](/projects/metasimfood/) and [DOMINO](/projects/domino/)
projects, Stéphane and Alice ran a stand on **fermented foods of today and
tomorrow**.

Visitors discussed familiar fermented foods such as yogurt, cheese, kefir,
kombucha and sauerkraut, and observed **lactic acid bacteria and the fungi
found in Camembert and Roquefort under the microscope**. These activities
offered a closer look at the microbial communities behind everyday foods.
